8.2 🌍 Earth's rotation right now — and what the magnetic field really does

Does the magnetic field steer the spin? No. Earth turns west → east (counter-clockwise seen from above the North Pole), so the Sun rises in the east everywhere. That angular momentum is left over from the planet's formation. The geomagnetic field is made by the churning iron outer core — it is a product of the rotation, not a motor for it. Tides lengthen the day by roughly 1.7 ms per century, and the atmosphere, oceans and core change the day length by milliseconds. Space weather cannot flip or speed up the planet.

The direct beam is what a panel pointed straight at the Sun would receive above the atmosphere (1361 W/m² scaled by the actual Earth–Sun distance). Flat-ground values multiply it by sin(elevation) and ignore clouds. Sunrise and sunset use the standard solar-geometry formula and are usually within a few minutes of official times.

8.3 🌞 Sunlight on every planet + other planets' pull — computed now

How much sunlight each planet gets right now

Distances are computed from Keplerian elements for this minute.
Irradiance is 1361 W/m² × (1 AU ÷ distance)². Mercury's close orbit means about ten times Earth's sunlight; Neptune, thirty times farther out, gets about a thousandth. Click any row to hear it — closer to the Sun, higher the pitch.

Other planets' pull on Earth

Real numbers, honest scale: bars compare each tidal pull with the Moon's. The Sun manages about half the Moon's tides; Venus and Jupiter are less than a thousandth of a percent. Planetary alignments therefore do not measurably change Earth's rotation, its tides or earthquakes. The computed pulls use today's actual distances.

How it works
  • Every frame is scanned for average colour, brightness, where the light sits, and frame-to-frame change.
  • Warmth picks the note on the scene scale, brightness sets loudness and filter opening, position sets stereo pan, and motion fires the percussion.
  • Sound is synthesised live with the Web Audio API — there are no recorded tracks.
  • Still images are swept by a moving scan meridian, so the picture is heard from west to east.
  • The 3D globe uses WebGL and an embedded land mask. NASA imagery requires a network connection.
  • The Scientific Lab adds public measurements next to the art: live air quality, climate and sea-level curves, USGS earthquakes, and a satellite comparison graph — every value also sonified.
  • The Space weather, planets & satellites panel reads NOAA SWPC space weather, computes sunlight on every planet and the planets' pull on Earth, and predicts ISS passes over your location from a public TLE — all in the browser.

Data & disclosures

The four built-in scenes are procedurally generated demo visuals inspired by NASA EIC / SVS datasets (sea surface currents, GEOS aerosols, night lights, ISS lightning) — they are not raw NASA data. NASA GIBS supplies satellite imagery and precipitation visualizations on request; their pixels, not raw scientific measurements, drive the sound. Imagery may be delayed, incomplete or unavailable. The globe and sound are artistic representations, not scientific measurements. We acknowledge the use of imagery provided by NASA's Global Imagery Browse Services (GIBS), part of NASA's Earth Science Data and Information System (ESDIS). Himawari imagery is from JMA; GOES imagery is from NOAA.

Scientific Lab sources: air quality and pollutants from Open-Meteo (CAMS), earthquakes from USGS, climate and sea-level series from NASA GISS and the Mauna Loa Keeling record, and the satellite comparison is a curated catalogue of operational free-data missions (NASA, ESA Copernicus, NOAA, JMA) with their published resolutions and refresh times — a catalogue, not live telemetry.

Space weather, planets and passes: solar wind, IMF, Kp, Dst and the NOAA storm scale come from NOAA SWPC public JSON; the ISS two-line element set comes from api.wheretheiss.at. Planet positions, sunlight, gravitational pull, sidereal time, solar elevation and satellite passes are computed in your browser from published Keplerian elements and a J2 orbit model — useful approximations, not official ephemerides or navigation data. Space weather does not measurably change Earth's rotation or axis; the geomagnetic field is a product of the core dynamo, not a driver of the spin. Pass times can be a minute or two off; confirm with NASA Spot the Station before travelling.

Files stay in your browser and are never uploaded. Team MSB Creative Studios · NASA Space Apps Challenge 2026 — public NASA data only, no analytics or tracking. Super Intelligence tools assisted with code and documentation; mapping logic and creative direction are by the team.

MSB NASA EARTH VIEW — help

Quick start

Load a NASA GIBS satellite or precipitation image, pick a simulated demo, or load your own image or video, then press Start sonification. The scanning meridian sweeps across the globe and the colours, brightness and motion underneath it become sound.

What you are hearing

  • Colour → pitch — cool blues sit low and deep, warm reds and ambers climb high.
  • Brightness → volume — dark regions are soft, bright regions are loud and open the filter.
  • Position → stereo pan — where the light sits moves the sound across the stereo field.
  • Motion → rhythm — frame-to-frame change and lightning flashes fire percussion.

Sound design

The Sound design panel switches the synth voice and the scale without changing the data mapping. Holographic is the scene-defined default, Jukebox lite restores the original lite engine (saw pad, triangle lead, 0.34 s delay network and the 1800 Hz noise-crack drum), Cinematic drone slows the glides and deepens the reverb, and Kinetic storm tightens the rhythm. The scale menu can force a minor pentatonic, Lydian, Dorian, natural minor or whole-tone set over any scene, and percussion or bells can be muted independently.

Live Earth & Scientific Lab

Live Earth checks public data straight from your browser — NASA EONET open disasters, NASA GIBS availability and the live ISS position (wheretheiss.at) — and draws the orbiting satellite fleet on the globe. The Scientific Lab below it is now one merged page instead of tabs: 7.1 Air Quality (Open-Meteo AQI, PM2.5 / PM10 / O₃ / NO₂ / CO, “Use my location”), 7.2 Climate Lab (1880 → 2024 comparison, Keeling CO₂ curve, CSV export), 7.3 Water & Ice (sea-level curve, GRACE and IMERG tips), 7.4 Hazards (USGS earthquakes in the last 24 hours) and 7.5 Satellite comparison — a bar chart ranking ten operational free/public missions by spatial detail, update speed or openness. Click any bar to hear that mission; the whole catalogue exports as CSV.

Space weather, planets & satellites over you

The 8. Space weather, planets & satellites panel answers questions the rest of the app does not:

  • 8.1 Live magnetosphere — solar wind speed, IMF Bz and Bt, Kp, Dst and the NOAA G-scale from NOAA SWPC public JSON, refreshed every five minutes. It estimates whether aurora could reach your magnetic latitude and what the storm means for radio, GPS and power grids.
  • 8.2 Earth's rotation — sidereal time, spin speed at your latitude, orbital speed, your magnetic latitude and a dipole estimate of the field. It also states the honest answer: the magnetic field is made by the core and does not steer or change Earth's rotation. Solar elevation, direct sunlight and sunrise/sunset for your place are computed locally.
  • 8.3 Planets — how much sunlight every planet receives right now (in W/m² and as a percentage of Earth's), plus the gravitational and tidal pull each planet has on Earth compared with the Moon. Click any row to hear it.
  • 8.4 Over your place — press 📍 Use my location, then 🔭 Find ISS passes. The app fetches the public ISS two-line element set, propagates the orbit in your browser and lists passes for the next three days. Click a pass to draw its sky path, add it to your calendar, or hear it. A public TLE for another satellite can be pasted at the bottom.

Accessibility

Everything is reachable by keyboard and announced through ARIA live regions: the current scene, play state, note and motion are read out as they change. The header offers a daylight theme (following your system preference until you choose otherwise) and a high-contrast mode, and the page honours prefers-reduced-motion. The audio engine starts only after a user gesture, as browsers require.

Privacy

No analytics or CDN. NASA imagery is requested directly from NASA GIBS. Local images and videos are decoded in your browser and never leave the device.
